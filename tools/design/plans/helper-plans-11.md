## #1020 The held-message queue is uncapped while frozen or busy

**Reproduced.** The owner is exporting (`FM._exporting = true`) and an Editor sends one tx every 25 ms for 4 s: **154 txs sent, 154 held** in `S._queued()`, no cap at all; it rises at the token-bucket rate and only the host's limit (30 tx/s, burst 60) bounds it.
**Where (Read).** `const msgQueue = []` at `js/collab-session.js:117`. The OWNER pushes `{kind:'live', ops, by, ord}` at `:625` (inside `case 'tx'`, after `host.receive` has already applied the tx to `host.base` and `broadcast` has sent it on); a GUEST pushes whole messages `{kind:'msg', msg}` at `:817`. Drain: `drainQueue` (`:1282-1291`), called from `S.tick` (`:1268`), returns while `frozen() || busy()`. `frozen()` is `FM._exporting` (`js/collab-bridge.js:176`), `busy()` is a running job or a muted history (`:177`). The only limiter on the owner's side is the host token bucket, `TX_PER_SEC: 30, TX_BURST: 60` per member (`js/collab-core.js:60`, `takeToken` `js/collab-host.js:338`), and one tx may be up to `TX_BYTES` 4 MB and `TX_OPS` 5000 (`:53`). **Worst case by arithmetic (Computed):** 30 tx/s x 4 MB = 120 MB/s per hostile Editor for as long as the export runs. Honest use: tens of small txs a second for a few minutes.
**Callers traced, and why "drop" is not one line.** The two queues are different things. The owner's entries are live applications of ops that are **already in `host.base`** and already broadcast: dropping one leaves the owner's live document behind its own base, for ever (nothing re-applies it). The guest's entries are whole messages (batches `b`, acks, snapshots, a `tail`): dropping one opens a gap in `S.bs` (batch sequence) and loses an ack (the outstanding tx never leaves `outstanding`). Existing overflow door for guests: `sendToHost({ t: 'resync', seq: S.bs, h: S.baseHash() })` (`:901`, `:929`, `:1053`), answered by the owner's `catchUp` (`:771`), which is already budgeted. For the owner the door is `D.diffDoc(view(), host.base)` applied the way `onSnap` does for a guest (`:1005-1015`).
**Build.**
1. A shared `held(entry)` helper counting entries and bytes (`canon(ops).length` for live, the message's JSON length for msg) against `LIM.HELD_MSGS` (say 2,000) and `LIM.HELD_BYTES` (say 16 MB; same order as `RING_BYTES` 8 MB).
2. Owner overflow: set `overflow = true`, clear the queue, and remember to reconcile; in `drainQueue`, if `overflow`, apply `diffDoc(view(), host.base)` to live (the `onSnap` live-diff code, factored out) then clear the flag. Guest overflow: clear the queue, send one `resync`, and ignore batches until the snap arrives (the existing `S.bs`/hash path handles the rest).
3. Say it once (toast "Caught up after a long export") only if something was dropped.
**Test.** Owner with `FM._exporting = true`, an Editor feeding txs (a fake clock for the token bucket, or `await sleep` as in my repro): `S._queued()` stays under the cap; unfreeze; `S.hash()` equals `S.baseHash()` (the entry's check) and the Editor's last value is on screen. A guest variant: queue past the cap, unfreeze, assert one `resync` sent and the document converges after the snapshot. **Mutations:** drop the owner's reconcile (the hash assertion goes red); never clear (the cap assertion goes red).
**Effort:** three hours (the owner's reconcile is the work). **Risk:** medium, because it touches the only code that keeps the owner's live copy equal to base; the hash assertion is the guard. **Check in ChatGPT's commit:** a plain `msgQueue.shift()` on overflow is **the bug, not the fix** on the owner's side (silent divergence); also that welcome stays unqueued (`:817`).

## #1023 Ordinary toasts are not announced

**Reproduced.** After `FM.toast('P11 toast probe')` there is no `#toast-sr` at all, and `#toast` carries no role and no `aria-live` (the role is removed at `js/app.js:1397`).
**Where (Read).** `FM.toast` (`js/app.js:1393`) writes `#toast`'s `textContent` and **removes** `role` and `tabindex` on every call (`:1397`), setting them only for the tappable variant. `#toast` is a plain div (`index.html:638`). **375 `FM.toast(` occurrences in `js/` today** (the entry says 244; the three-argument form is the only exception).
**Callers traced, and two traps.** (1) Not every toast goes through `FM.toast`: `js/collab-ui.js:3890` and `js/medialib.js:262` take `#toast` directly, and `FM.hideToast` (`js/app.js:1581`) is separate; a mirror inside `FM.toast` misses those two. (2) **A toast is relabelled many times a second** ("Preparing frames… 45%", "Tracking… 12%", the comment at `:1427-1431` says so), so a live region fed on every call would be read as a stream. The entry's "written on the next frame" does not address it.
**Build.**
1. A permanent visually hidden `#toast-sr` (`role="status"`, `aria-live="polite"`, `aria-atomic="true"`) in `index.html` beside `#toast`, **not** on `#toast` (the tappable branch swaps its role, as the entry says).
2. In `FM.toast`: `announce(msg)`, which writes `#toast-sr` at most once per 1.5 s and skips a message whose digits-stripped text equals the last one's (so a progress toast announces its first line and then nothing until the words change). A longer message ends a pending shorter one.
3. Route the two direct writers (`collab-ui.js:3890`, `medialib.js:262`) through `FM.toast`, or call `FM.announce` from them.
**Test.** `FM.toast('hello')` puts "hello" in `#toast-sr` after a frame; 30 calls of `'Preparing frames… ' + i + '%'` within 2 s produce at most two writes; the tappable toast keeps `role=button` on `#toast` (control). **Mutation:** remove the digit dedupe (the burst assertion goes red).
**Effort:** an hour. **Risk:** none for sighted users.

## #1024 The keyframe diamonds are named only "◆" and expose no state

**Reproduced, narrowly.** Opening the Move & Transform card on a shape with two effects: of the 4 keyframe buttons my sweep reached, **1** was named only by its glyph (`mt-kf title="Add a keyframe at the playhead"`); the sweep reaches only the cards it opens, so the **13 sites** below come from reading the code, not from this run.
**Where (Read) and a correction.** The entry lists four sites in the effect builders (`js/inspector.js:1137, 1346, 1378, 1408`). **There are 13 creation sites**, in five families: `fx-kf` (`:1137, :1346, :1378, :1408`), `kf-btn` (`:45`, the shared property row, and `:3182`, the mask path), `mt-kf` (`:4652` crop, `:4824` points, `:4925` position, `:5603` volume, `:5930` speed), the `fill-kf` diamond (`:4155`, whose glyph toggles `◆`/`◇` at `:4159, :4224`) and `mt-vbox-kf` (`:4452-4465`, **which already has an `aria-label`**). Every one already sets a good `title` carrying the state in words ("Remove volume keyframe at playhead", "Keyframe the speed at the playhead (speed ramp)"), but a `title` is only a description when the button has text, and the text here is "◆", so the accessible name is the glyph. The `fx-kf` and `kf-btn` titles lack the parameter name ("Animate this parameter").
**Callers traced.** Each builder repaints its button's `title` and classes on every refresh (`here`, `active`); that repaint is the one place a state label can be kept right. Three states, as the entry says: none, animated elsewhere (`active`), a keyframe here (`here`).
**Build.** One helper `kfLabel(btn, name, state)` setting `aria-label` (and `title`, so the two cannot disagree) to "Add <name> keyframe at playhead" / "Remove <name> keyframe at playhead" / "<name> is animated; add a keyframe at playhead", and `aria-pressed = state==='here'`. Call it in the 12 builders without a label, **inside each repaint** (not only at creation), passing the parameter's own label (`p.label`, `labelText`, 'volume', 'speed', 'crop', 'position', 'points', 'mask path'). Mark the glyph `aria-hidden` by wrapping it in a span.
**Test.** The entry's: one control from each of the five families, name contains the parameter and flips Add to Remove after a click (**re-acquire the button**, the inspector rebuilds on click), `aria-pressed` follows; plus a sweep asserting no `.fx-kf, .kf-btn, .mt-kf, .fill-kf` button on the open card is named by the glyph alone. **Mutation:** set the label only at creation (the flip assertion goes red).
**Effort:** two hours, mostly the repaint call sites. **Risk:** none.
