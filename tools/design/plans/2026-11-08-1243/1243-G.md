**2. What's there now**

- **The logger's lead is half right.** Bone is the first colour iOS samples. But iOS does not simply read "a fixed full-screen element's colour, else the root's". The measured WebKit rule (plan.md §3.1):
  1. It hit-tests the top centre, 4 px down, walks up to the first fixed or sticky box, and takes the first plain `background-color` on the way.
  2. **A viewport-sized container (or a dimming layer or sidebar) never replaces a colour that is already set** (WebKit 8b209a7, iOS 26.0).
  3. If nothing fixed is found, the last colour is kept while its container is still rendered. Otherwise there is no colour, and iOS draws its soft-edge **blur** over the top ~45 pt.
  4. It looks again **only** when a fixed or sticky renderer is added or removed. A change of colour or class does not make it look again.

- **Why cream (from reading, not measured on his phone).**
  1. On a cold launch the first container WebKit finds is `#splash`, which is bone (index.html:121).
  2. The flip to coal at 2.14 s is a class change (ground(), index.html:841-847), so the strip stays cream over the black end frame.
  3. When `#splash` is removed (index.html:882), `#home-screen` is found, but it is viewport-sized, so rule 2 keeps bone.
  4. Into a project, `#app` goes fixed and full-screen (styles.css:7680), and rule 2 keeps bone again. After the slide nothing fixed is at the top, and rule 3 keeps bone because `#app` is still rendered.
  5. So bone stays for the whole session, on Home and in projects. The same rule kept #111 all session on 26 Sep (plan.md §3.3, run 2) and gave him #883's white bar in a project.
- **Why it swaps with the blur (G1).** When the last container is `display:none` (Home hidden with no fixed `#app`: reduced motion, or the desktop-gated swap at home.js:598-599), WebKit has no colour. iOS 26 then lays its soft edge over the top ~45 pt, over the buttons. Which of the two he gets depends on the order of screens that session.
- **The logger's step 1 would not fix it.** Recolouring `#splash`'s background-color to the hand-over screen makes that colour the session's first, and rule 2 then locks it in. After a light Home, the strip would be #fafdff over every project, which is #883 again. During the take it would also be off-colour over 2.14 s of bone.

**3. The change: one invisible tab per strip colour, and exactly one shown at a time (CSS only, no JS)**
1. **Add the tabs.** index.html: first thing in `<body>` (before line 95), add five empty `<i class="fm-edge" data-edge="bone|coal|home-light|home-dark|editor" aria-hidden="true"></i>`, then `<style id="edge-css">`.
   - Base rule: `.fm-edge{display:none;position:fixed;left:0;right:0;top:0;height:12px;z-index:10001;pointer-events:none;-webkit-mask-image:linear-gradient(transparent,transparent);mask-image:linear-gradient(transparent,transparent)}`.
   - Why each part: 12 px because WebKit skips boxes ≤10 px. Full width so it is ≥90 % of the viewport. Opacity 1 because WebKit skips <0.1. The mask paints 0 px, and WebKit still reads it (measured 26 Sep; clip-path and 0-height wrappers are NOT read). WebKit's first hit-test ignores pointer-events. z 10001 puts it above `#splash`. And it is NOT viewport-sized, so WebKit lets it replace any colour.
