## #1056 Mosaic's Average mode gives cutout edges a dark fringe (LOW, **needs his OK**)
**Where (Read):** `js/compositor.js` `PIXEL_FX.mosaic` (starts at `:6679` on main). The block loop sums `moS[i]`, `moS[i+1]`, `moS[i+2]`, `moS[i+3]` and divides each by the pixel count `moN`: a plain mean of straight-alpha colour. The plate holds 0,0,0 under zero alpha (the comment at `:6476` records it, measured 5 Sep), so every transparent pixel in a tile drags the colour toward black while the alpha is averaged correctly.
**Callers traced:** `PIXEL_FX.mosaic` is reached only through the table: `drawPixelEffect(ctx, layer, t, scene, fx, PIXEL_FX[fx.type])` (`:3867`, a layer's Mosaic effect, preview and export alike since the exporter calls the same compositor), the no-op probe `fxVerdict` (`:4368`) and the effect thumbnails (`js/fx-thumbs.js`, which runs the same kernels). Nothing else sums colour this way.
**What happens (Measured):** a white disc on transparent, Mosaic size 16: the edge tiles come out 128,128,128 at alpha 128 (half-covered tile). A white cutout gets a **grey** rim, not a lighter edge. Picture: `results/p18_1056_before_after.png`.
**Plan (patch, 18 lines):** weight each pixel's colour by its own alpha, `Σ(c·a) / Σa`, and keep the alpha mean as it is; a tile with `Σa = 0` stays 0. **Opaque plates do not change by a single byte**: every weight is 255, so the quotient is the same rational number as the old one (255·Σc over 255·n), and both are correctly rounded from the same real number. The test proves it against the old flat mean on a gradient plate.
**What he must say yes to:** *saved projects with Mosaic Average on a layer that has transparent edges will render a lighter rim after this*. There is no setting; it is what the effect should have always done. The picture shows exactly the change. If he says no, close the item with the picture as the record.
**Test:** the white cutout (opaque tile white, transparent tile alpha 0, half tile alpha about 128 and colour **white**), Centre pixel unchanged (its centre pixel is transparent), and a byte-for-byte comparison on an opaque 64 by 32 plate. Red on main (the rim is 128), green patched. Mutations (Measured): all-weights-255 reverted the fix, CAUGHT (the alpha CONTROL tripped first); the alpha mean replaced, CAUGHT. **One mutant is equivalent and I did not try to kill it:** the `Σa > 0 ? … : 0` guard against dividing by zero is cosmetic, because `NaN` written into a `Uint8ClampedArray` is 0 anyway.

That is the whole reason this is a plan and not a patch.
**What the cost is (Measured, desktop headless Chromium 141, the renderer process's resident memory, one 12 MP JPEG of 4.6 MB loaded through `loadImageFile` and drawn small, N separate loads):** the first decoded still costs **+47 MB**; five cost **+237 MB**; ten cost **+434 MB**; at twenty the browser's decode cache starts evicting and the figure is **+385 MB**, not more. That touches the compositor's image draw and the crop and tracker readers, not the 139 sites. (3) The test the entry gives: a 6000 by 4000 PNG in a 1080 by 1920 project gives a preview source at or below the cap, and the export frame is byte-identical to a full-resolution render.

## #1059 Detect speech gives up on the first clip with no sound, so the talking clip is never tried (MEDIUM)
**Where (Read):** the click handler in `js/captions.js` `detectRow` (loop from `:528` on main): on the two whole-scope modes it builds a queue `[chosen, …the rest]` and loops `r = await C.detect(layer, cand, …)`; `C.detect` (`:276`) throws `no decodable audio in that clip` when `FM.decodeAudio` returns null, and **that throw leaves the whole `try`**: the catch says *Speech detection failed* and the rest of the queue is never tried. The comment above the loop says the opposite is intended (*"the next clip might be the voice track"*).
**Callers traced:** `C.detect` has one caller, that loop (`:531`). `C.audioSources()` (`:174`) lists every video layer with a media file and does not look at whether it has sound. `FM.hasAudioTrack(layer)` (`js/media.js:937`) answers `true`, `false` or `null` for free from what is already known.
**What happens (Measured, the test on main):** a project with a silent clip first and a talking clip second, scope *The whole project*: toast *"Speech detection failed — the details are in Settings → Last error → Copy"*; no cue is made.
**Plan (patch, 45 lines):** `C.detect` marks both of its no-sound throws `noAudio`; the loop (a) skips a clip `FM.hasAudioTrack` already says has none, without decoding it, (b) catches a `noAudio` throw and carries on, rethrowing anything else; (c) when **every** clip tried was silent it says one plain line (*"None of these clips has any sound to listen to"*, or *"“name” has no sound to listen to"* for a single clip) instead of the failure toast. The *One audio clip…* scope keeps its meaning (he named the clip, so only that clip is tried) and now gets the plain line instead of the failure toast.
**Test:** three scenarios on the real button with `FM.decodeAudio` and `FM.detectSpeech` stubbed: (1) first clip decodes to null, second talks: a cue toast and no failure; (2) first clip's record says `hasAudioTrack: false`: it is **not decoded** (the stub records what it was asked to decode); (3) both silent: the plain line and no failure. Red on main at (1). Mutations (Measured): the `noAudio` catch removed, CAUGHT; the free pre-check removed, CAUGHT; the all-silent line removed, CAUGHT.

## #1061 A hold speed key that drops sharply leaves a lasting footage offset, and a split changes it (LOW)
**Where (Read):** `js/scene.js` `FM.layerSourceAdvance` (`:952` on main): a cumulative table at 120 Hz by trapezoid, `acc += (prev + v) / (2 * SR)`. A hold key inside a sample interval books the average of the old and new speed for the whole interval, and the table keeps that for the rest of the clip.
**Callers traced:** `layerSourceAdvance` feeds `FM.layerLocalTime` (so preview, scrub and export), the audio paths (`js/audio-play.js:63-69`, `js/audio-fx-live.js:169-173`), the split (`js/app.js:4924-4925`) and `FM.maxDurForSource` (`js/scene.js:1008-1015`), which bisects it. **They all share the one table, so preview and export always agree with each other; the error is in all of them equally.**
**What happens (Measured, `p18_probe2.py` on main):** speed 100 holding until 0.5033 s, then 1: the advance at 1.5 s is **51.4125 s of source against an exact 51.3300** (0.0825 s off, 2.5 frames at 30 fps). A split at +0.3041 s, then the two halves summed against the whole clip at 1.2 s: **-0.419 s** (a split should be invisible; this one moves the footage by 12 frames). **A correction to VERIFIED.md's example:** its test splits at +0.25 s, which is exactly 30 samples, so the two grids coincide and a split there shows **0 difference** (Measured: 0.000). The split has to land off the 120 Hz grid to show the problem; the test uses +0.3041.
**Plan (patch, 28 lines):** when a keyframe falls strictly inside a sample interval, split that trapezoid at the key: the left limit (`evalProp` just before the key) on the left part and the key's own value on the right part. Intervals with no key inside use the old expression, so a ramp is unchanged before its first interior key (the table is a `Float32Array`, which also hides ulp differences, so I treat the `lt === t0` branch as documentation and did not try to kill that mutant). **Not byte-identical for smooth ramps with a key off the grid** (a hair, in the key's own interval); that goes in the POLISH-LOG line.
**Test:** the entry's own clip (100x until a hold step to 1x at +0.5033) within 1 ms of `100·ts + 1·(1.5 - ts)`; the misaligned split within 1 ms of the unsplit clip; a plain linear ramp unchanged; and the table before the first interior key equal to the old trapezoid, copied into the test. Red on main (0.0825 s off). Patched: 51.3300 against an exact 51.3300, and the split difference **1.3e-6 s**. Mutations (Measured): the key split removed, the left limit replaced by the key's own value, and the key list emptied: all CAUGHT.
