People shapes, from real signs

#929, your words: the human shapes are still bad. Make sure you reference other shapes that you can find online and try and make them look like it.

Every round before this (v3.49, v6.03, #160, #435, #760) reshaped the same home-made figure. These three are traced from real, published pictograms, each one free to use. All of it is drawn by the app itself: big on light and dark, then the real Add → Shape tiles, at the size they appear on your phone (380 wide) and on PC (1280 wide).

A · The airport sign(recommended)

AIGA / US DOT symbol signs, 1974 — public domain. Traced from Wikimedia Commons Toilets_unisex.svg (PD-AIGA).

The sign on every US airport toilet door. Round shoulders, arms hanging beside the body with a thin slit, her arms angled out over an A-line dress. Changed from the sign, only so it survives the small tile: the neck gap, the arm slits and the leg gap are each a few units wider (17 / 13 / 12 of 572 instead of 13 / 10 / 9).

Breaks 6 of the 8 people tests:

  • his head touches his shoulders at 24px (it is clear at the real tile size: 29px on PC, 75px on your phone)
  • the arm slits are 1px at 51px — the old rule wants 2px
  • her arms angle out, so "same figure below the neck" and the arm-span band fail
  • it is 2 parts, not the 6 the #760 test counts

B · The map icon

Mapbox Maki toilet icon — CC0 (public domain).

Drawn for 15-pixel map pins, so it is chunkier: a bigger head, his arms are just the rounded ends beside his legs, hers angle out. Friendlier, less formal.

Breaks 6 of the 8 people tests:

  • head is 1 : 4.7 of the height (rule says 1 : 6 – 1 : 7)
  • his arms are part of his body, so there is no armpit gap to measure
  • at 48px her arm-to-dress wedge closes into small holes
  • not 6 parts; not the same figure below the neck

C · Google’s app icon

Google Material Symbols man / woman (Rounded) — Apache 2.0.

The Android / Google look: no arms and one leg column, on purpose. Cleanest at small sizes — but you asked for arms in #160, and this takes them away.

Breaks 6 of the 8 people tests:

  • no arms (the #160 rule)
  • one leg column, so "two legs at 24px" fails
  • head is 1 : 5
  • not 6 parts; not the same figure below the neck

About those tests

The 8 people tests lock in the home-made proportions: 6 separate parts, wide gaps sized for 24px, a 1 : 6 – 1 : 7 head. Any real sign breaks 6 of them. If you pick one, I rewrite those 6 around the sign you picked (the outline has to match it; head, arms and legs have to read at the real tile size) instead of the old numbers. The two that stay as they are: mirror-symmetric, and a round head.
