# AU19 the timeline's media thumbnails and filmstrips

Branch `hunt/audit-filmstrip`, against main fba44ca8 (v17.32). Labels: Measured / Read / Guess. Probes and raw results in `audit-filmstrip-scripts/`. Headless Chromium on Linux; the clips are WebM/VP9 recorded in the page (this container has no H.264, so the shipped `splash.mp4` and the tests that need it cannot run here).

## Finding AU19-1 (MEDIUM, fixed): after Undo or Redo of a replaced video, the clip bar shows the OTHER clip's frames
- **Measured** (`replace3.js`): a red clip on the timeline, then the real Replace-media sequence with a blue clip, then Undo, then Redo. The record under the layer and the strip's own frames are right each time, but **the canvas drawn on the bar is the wrong clip's: after the replace it is blue (right), after Undo the clip is red and the bar stays blue, after Redo the clip is blue and the bar shows red.** It stays wrong through every later rebuild until the width, trim or duration changes. The same canvas element survives each `FM.timeline.rebuild()`, which is what a cache hit looks like.
- **Cause (Measured, by logging every cache lookup).** `timeline.js` caches each clip's strip canvas by layer id with a key made of `width | trimStart | duration | frame count | layer.mediaRev`. After an Undo the layer's JSON is back at revision 0, but the registry still holds the NEW file for a moment (`restoreReplacedMedia` lands after the history step). In that gap the timeline drew the blue clip's frames and filed them under the key for revision 0. When the red file's own frames arrived, their key was the same, so they hit the blue canvas. Redo is the same mechanism the other way round. The key named the layer's revision, not the revision of the file the frames came from.
- **Fix** (`js/timeline.js`, buster 261 to 262): the key reads the RECORD's revision (`m.rev`, which a replace and a restore both stamp) and falls back to the layer's when a record has none, the same expression `storage.js` 546 uses to say which revision a record is.
- **Proof.** `AU19-1` builds a red and a blue WebM in the page, then drives the exact replace sequence from `FM.replaceMedia` and checks both the record's first strip frame and the canvas actually on the bar, after the first build, after the replace, after Undo and after Redo. **Red on main at 1280 and 380 ("after Undo the clip is red but its timeline bar shows blue frames"), green with the fix at both; 2 mutations CAUGHT** (the key reads the layer's revision again; the record's revision ignored). Neighbours: `686` (the image-replace strip bug this key was added for), `829` and `replace media` pass at both widths. The `strip`, `filmstrip` and `clip thumbnails` slices have 4 reds at 1280 and 380 that are **identical on main** (all need the shipped MP4, e.g. "splash.mp4 did not decode", or a VideoEncoder, which this container lacks): **untested here, not shown to pass.**

## What I measured and found sound
- **Memory (Measured, `strips.js`).** With `createImageBitmap` and `close` counted: 10 video clips build all their strips in **0.9 s**, one at a time through the global queue, each as 8 frames at 64 px tall (80 bitmaps live). Deleting every layer takes that to **0 live bitmaps** (the strip release in `teardownLayerPlayback`), and an Undo rebuilds only the restored clip's 8. Removing the projects takes it to 0.
- **Leaving a project (Measured).** With strips still being rebuilt for project A's clips, switching to a new project B and adding one clip: **B's first filmstrip is ready in 331 ms**, and A's bitmaps are gone (the live count is B's 8 only). This is the queue 690 "hunt f" behaviour (released clips are skipped and `emptied` ends a wait), confirmed rather than assumed.
- **Stale strip after a plain image replace.** Covered by the existing `686` test (key includes the revision); passes.
- **Bounds (Read).** The timeline's strip cache is capped at 40 entries; decoded strip frames are 64 px tall by design (about 230 KB a clip at 1080p source); `_extractStrip` sets `stripFrames` even to `[]` on failure so it never retries forever; `waitDecodable` has a 3 s cap and `seekAndPaint` a 500 ms cap, so one broken clip cannot stall the single queue for long.
