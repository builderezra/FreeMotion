# PF1 the "100 layers x 20 effects" freeze

Branch `hunt/perf-freeze`, against main fba44ca8 (v17.32). Labels: Measured / Read / Guess. Probes, raw outputs and the test are in `perf-freeze-scripts/`. Headless Chromium on Linux, software rendering, a 1080 x 1920 project, a 380 px phone viewport at 3x for the app numbers: **a phone is slower, so every figure is a floor.**

## Short version
The freeze has **two causes**. I fixed the first (it is where the time went at open); the second is real, measured, and is NOT fixed because the only exact fix I found changes the render path and needs your call. Stated as numbers: one layer with 8 Glow effects imported in **6.2 s before, 0.29 s after**; 10 Glow effects **14.2 s before, 0.62 s after**; two layers of 10 **27.6 s before, 0.9 s after**. The full 100 x 20 case still cannot be shown on screen in a reasonable time, for the second reason below, and I could not run it to the end.

## Cause 1 (fixed): the project card is rendered at full size, and the render is where the time is
- **Measured.** A CPU profile of `FM.storage.importObject` on one layer with 8 Glows (`prof.py`): **69 % of 9 s is `drawImage`**, all of it inside `makeThumb` (`js/storage.js` 2133), called from `touchCurrent` from `save()` and again directly from `importObject`. `renderScene` itself reports a few milliseconds, because a canvas draws lazily: the cost lands on the first readback (`drawImage` or `getImageData`), which is also why timing `renderScene` alone gave a false "this is fast" (I did that first and had to redo it with a flush).
- **Cause (Read).** `makeThumb` drew the whole scene onto a canvas of the PROJECT's size (1080 x 1920) and then halved it down to the 360 px card.
- **Fix.** Draw straight at twice the card's size using the compositor's own render scale (`__fmRS`, the way the phone's preview is drawn), then take the same last halving step. A project already within 360 px is untouched. `js/storage.js`, buster 58 to 59.
- **Measured effect (same scene, flushed):** a render of 8 Glows costs **3.0 s at 1080 x 1920, 0.58 s at the phone preview's size, 0.16 s at card size.** Import, one layer: 8 Glows 6.2 s to 0.29 s; 10 Glows 14.2 s to 0.62 s; two layers x 10 Glows 27.6 s to 0.9 s.
- **No picture changes.** The 482 pinned-picture slice is identical before and after, at 1280 and 380: the same 3 reds on main and on the branch (`482 2.6 Motion Blur (Object)…`, `482 3.0 Echo and Reverb…`, `482 6.7 Glow Scan…`, red on main before I touched anything) and 112 of 115 passing. The compositor was not touched. **What does change is the card's JPEG:** it is now drawn at card resolution, and on a detailed scene (shapes, text, a glow and a drop shadow, `thumbcmp.js`) the new card differs from the old by 0.61 mean absolute difference per channel and 1.2 % of pixels by more than 16, at the JPEG's own noise level. The `thumb`, `658`, `915`, `home`, `import` and `touchCurrent` slices have exactly the same reds on main as on the branch (`658 the thumbnail pin…`, `690 an export started while the timeline is still drawing…`, `915.5Br`, two `home` tests).

## Cause 2 (measured, NOT fixed): a stack of Glow effects is a chain of CSS filters whose cost grows roughly with the cube
- **Measured, `chain.js`.** The same N `drop-shadow(0 0 9.6px white)` stages on a 605 x 1075 canvas, as ONE `ctx.filter` list (what the compositor builds, `compositor.js` 2284) against N separate single-filter draws: **N = 4: 130 vs 64 ms; 8: 605 vs 121; 12: 1,643 vs 199; 16: 4,940 vs 351 ms.** The list is superlinear and the loop is linear. At the app, one layer of 20 Glows takes about 30 s for the first preview frame (`real.js`), and the card at 20 Glows still takes 5.6 s after the fix.
- **Cause (Guess, consistent with every number).** Chrome widens each stage's working region by that stage's blur reach, so stage k works on a region about k reaches wider than the canvas, and the total is about the sum of squares.
- **The fast path is exact only inside the frame (Measured).** Applying the stages one at a time gave **0 differing bytes** for content well inside the canvas, and **4,482 to 14,461 differing bytes (up to 255) when the content touches the plate edge**, because light that spilled past the edge in one stage re-enters in a later one in the chained version. So a loop of single stages is picture-identical only when the layer's alpha box widened by the total reach (N x ~3 sigma) stays inside the plate. At the phone's preview size that holds for small N and fails for large N (20 Glows at a 16 px radius is about 540 px of reach on a 605 px plate), which is exactly the case that freezes.
- **Why I did not do it.** It needs a guarded second render path in `drawLayer`, with the guard computed from the layer's alpha box, in the hottest code in the app, and it cannot help the case the entry names (20 effects). The honest options are (a) the guarded fast path for the cases it covers (stacks up to about 10, say, 4 to 14 times faster in my numbers); (b) the entry's own "open with effects paused and a banner" for stacks that are too heavy to draw, which changes the picture and, by his rule, needs designing and showing first; (c) both. Which, is a decision for the PM, not something to slip into a perf fix. I did not build either.

## The test (`PF1-test.js`, in `tests/tests.js`)
`PF1 importing a project with a heavy effect stack draws its card at the card's size, no full-size render, and the import stays within its time ceiling`: one shape layer with 8 Glow effects on a 1080 x 1920 project, imported through `importObject`; every `renderScene` call is recorded by canvas width and by whether the scene holds that layer. It asserts **no render of that layer at project size**, **at most one card render of it**, and a time ceiling of **2,500 ms** for the import (measured 6,200 ms before and 290 to 620 ms after; the ceiling is four times the worst "after" and well under the "before", so a slower machine passes and the old behaviour does not).
- **Red on main, green on the branch, at 1280 and 380** (`results/pf1_main_*`, `pf1_patched_*`): on main it fails with "opening the project rendered it at full size 1 time(s) (canvas widths 1080)".
- **Mutation CAUGHT:** the card drawn at project size again (`if (false) { src.width = tw * 2; … }`). I also tried a "dedupe the forced card capture" change and removed it: the second capture I expected was the OUTGOING project's card drawn by `create()`, which is correct, and its mutation survived, so it was unproven and is not in the patch.
- **The time ceiling is the weaker assertion** (a slower runner can only fail it by being four times slower than this one); the render-size assertions are the ones that pin the cause.
