Before this change, a simple shape moving from x=10 to x=110 at 4 holds/s showed the first held shape at x=4 after sequential renders at 0 and 0.2 s, but a cold render at 0.2 s showed the *current* shape at x=24. One new `{ item: 'TBD' }` regression in `tests/tests.js` failed `0/1` with those exact values before the patch. It also checks the inverse integrated clock for keyed Rate and the previous held shape in Trail mode after a cold seek.

The compositor now supplies an exact-time source sampler **only** for an ordinary vector shape with Frame Stutter as its sole effect, a non-media fill, no parent, masks, behaviors, or custom effect-clock offset. The sampler re-enters `drawLayer` at the quantum boundary with Frame Stutter removed, preserving that effect's stack position and avoiding recursion. Numeric Rate uses the existing arithmetic; keyed Rate inverts the already-integrated positive rate clock. Phase and Irregular determine the same quantum as before; Strobe's duty, Trail strength and predecessor, and Blend live are applied to the resulting samples. All other layers continue through the previous cached path.

Exact boundary sampling intentionally changes three #482 *shape* default pictures: Hold `cce99039 → e81c4609`, Strobe `6b0cf5fd → 6972d7c5`, Trail `aa1b606d → 9875e595`. Their 8 Hz boundaries at 0.125/0.25/0.375 s fall between 30 fps draws, so the old sequential cache held the first *later* drawn frame. The test's three shape goldens are updated with the old hashes recorded beside them; image goldens and all other defaults remain unchanged. A project-frame snap would preserve old hashes but sample after the true boundary and, on an off-grid seek, could read a future source frame. This is the deliberate visual tradeoff of fixing C31 for shapes.

Video needs a separate media-preparation path. `drawCanvasEffect` paints the live source plate at output time, and `drawLayer` for ordinary forward video reads `m.el` at its externally sought `currentTime` (`js/compositor.js:12748-12811,18768-18860`). Merely calling `drawLayer` with the earlier boundary time still reads the *current* video frame. Seeking that same element to the boundary upstream also fails: Blend live needs the current frame at the same render, Trail needs a previous boundary frame, and the element may be driving audible native playback. The existing whole-clip `frameCache` is bounded/downscaled for preview and capped at 900 frames for export, so it is not a pixel-exact general replacement for long or 4K footage.

The least invasive complete design is a render-scoped source override keyed by layer/effect and source time. Prepare bounded decoded snapshots for the current, held, and prior boundary frames without seeking the playback element, then let the existing `drawLayer(tmp, boundaryProjectTime)` take the appropriate snapshot while evaluating earlier *deterministic* effects and transforms at that time. A quiet, separate media decoder (or a validated frame-cache hit) can prepare preview snapshots asynchronously and request a repaint only if scene/playhead generation still matches. `FM.setTime` itself renders synchronously (`js/app.js:1718-1743`), so it cannot promise a completed historical video frame on return; the UI needs an explicit pending-frame behavior. Export must await full-resolution snapshots before each synchronous render, under its existing element/cancel ownership (`js/exporter.js:263-274,1358-1384,1800-1806,1874-1880`). The Worker path must transfer those snapshots alongside its current `VideoFrame` (`js/export-worker.js:43-103,479-501,672-720`), close them on every path, and revise the worker/resume identity because output pixels change. Do not land a compositor-only video redraw. Upstream temporal effects, stacked Frame Stutters, and animated Phase/Irregular crossing semantics need separate defined behavior before expanding this gate.
